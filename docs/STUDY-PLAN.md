# Study plans — CT-PT

Two schedules. Pick by how much time you have, not by how confident you feel.

Both are ordered **by points-per-hour, not by chapter number** — with one deliberate exception.
Starting at Chapter 1 and grinding forward is tempting, and Chapter 1 genuinely *is* the best value
in the syllabus, so it stays first. But Chapter 3 — the syllabus's second-longest chapter for only
7 marks — must not sit in front of you while you are fresh, and **Chapter 4 must never be the thing
you run out of time for**. It is half the paper.

**The single planning fact that matters:** the pass mark is **26 of 40**, and Chapters 1, 2, 3 and 5
together are only **20 marks**. **Chapter 4 is not optional.**

## What "covered" means

A chapter is covered when you can, without notes:

- **Chapter 1** — define all eight performance testing types in one line each, place any dynamic
  activity at its test level, name the four load generation options, and map a cause to its failure
  mode.
- **Chapter 2** — classify any metric as technical, business or operational, separate the three
  metric sources by verb, state why results are aggregated, and map an outcome back to its test
  type.
- **Chapter 3** — name the risk profile of all nine architectures, say which activity owns which
  task, and give both K4 answer patterns (**repeatedly throughout the lifecycle**; **static review
  plus dynamic test**).
- **Chapter 4** — recite the seven protocol groups, the twelve PTP sections, the three script
  sections, the throughput formula and the seven analysis items; and perform the load-profile and
  concurrency calculations cold.
- **Chapter 5** — separate the three tool types by verb, name the three licence models, and run the
  four-factor elimination on a tool selection scenario.

## Daily habits that matter more than the schedule

1. **Flashcards every day**, even on rest days. Ten minutes. Three clean passes per chapter before
   exam day.
2. **Rebuild one table from blank paper each session.** Recognition is not recall, and the exam
   tests recall.
3. **Keep a one-page error list.** Every question you get wrong, in one line: the objective, the
   trap, the correct rule. By the last week this page is your entire revision.
4. **Read the official syllabus alongside Section 1.** The guides quote it heavily, but the exam is
   written from the source document, and its phrasing is what appears in the options.
